# Varia Studio — Sales Playbook v2
**Effective:** 14 November 2026 · **Model:** Service-led, product-supported engineering studio

## 1. Company proposition
Varia Studio is an independent, founder-led applied AI and geospatial engineering studio for agriculture, soil and environmental data.

We help existing technical businesses add a missing engineering capability to an active project:
- computer-vision and ML evaluation/integration;
- geospatial data engineering and remote-sensing workflows;
- soil and environmental data pipelines;
- backend APIs, dashboards and decision-support software.

**Commercial thesis:** sell a bounded engineering outcome to a company with an existing customer, project or operational bottleneck. Do not ask prospects to fund Varia's internal product validation.

Product status:
- Varia Vision: experimental RGB aerial-image weed-detection prototype; current deployed model is single-class; no independent customer-field validation.
- Varia Carbon: research prototype; provenance and validation require remediation; not for MRV, carbon-credit issuance or high-stakes soil decisions.
- Trait-Bridge: research direction; parked until a buyer problem and paid use case exist.

## 2. Ideal customer profile
**Primary:** AgTech, drone-data, GIS and geospatial service firms that already sell services/software, have active agriculture/soil/environment/remote-sensing projects, show a specific data/software bottleneck, and have an identifiable buyer and plausible procurement route.

**Secondary:** soil/environment consultancies and agricultural software vendors with a specific funded data or integration workflow.

**Buyer roles:** Founder/CEO/Managing Director; CTO/Head of Engineering; Head of Geospatial/Remote Sensing/GIS; Head of Product/Data/AI; Delivery Director; Programme Technology Lead.

**Deprioritize:** direct competitors with complete in-house capability and no complementary gap; research-only groups without a funded commercial route; large enterprises with no project sponsor; companies with no evidence of active projects or budget; prospects seeking free custom reports or unpaid integration.

## 3. Offers
### A. Applied AI / Geospatial Engineering Sprint
For an existing project with a delivery bottleneck. Potential scope: data ingestion/cleaning, raster/vector processing, remote-sensing workflows, ML integration, backend/API, dashboards, documentation and handover. Commercial unit: fixed-scope implementation sprint.

### B. Technical Diagnostic
For an unclear workflow or data/integration decision. Deliverables may include workflow map, data-readiness review, feasibility/architecture assessment, prioritized plan and risk register. Commercial unit: paid diagnostic.

### C. API / Data Integration
For a product/service firm with an existing platform and a specific integration need. Scope may include API design, pipeline integration, model endpoint integration, spatial database and dashboard integration. Begin with paid discovery when requirements are unclear. Do not promise SLA, uptime or production support before verifying infrastructure and operating costs.

### Varia OS route mapping
- `audit` = paid technical diagnostic.
- `pilot` = fixed-scope paid engineering sprint / proof of concept.
- `api_white_label` = API, backend or data integration discovery.
- `discovery_pending` = need, buyer or budget not yet established.

The route is a hypothesis until the buyer confirms the need. Use product value `Other` for general studio-service leads.

## 4. Qualification score
Score each criterion 0–5; missing evidence receives zero.
1. Problem evidence — specific workflow or delivery gap.
2. Urgency — active project, deadline, expansion or funded programme.
3. Budget evidence — paying customers, commercial contracts, procurement or funding.
4. Buyer access — named economic/technical buyer or verified contact route.
5. Delivery fit — Varia can deliver the bounded work with current capability.

Triage: 18–25 first-wave priority; 15–17 research the missing factor; 0–14 nurture/disqualify. This is a research priority score, not a conversion probability and not buyer qualification.

## 5. Natasha operating contract
Natasha may research companies, collect source URLs and factual evidence, identify likely buyer roles, recommend an offer route and draft one-to-one messages.

Natasha must:
- distinguish buyers, integration partners, competitors, vendors and research organisations;
- exclude competitors unless a specific complementary gap is evidenced;
- never infer email addresses or invent budget, urgency, revenue, customer counts, ROI or buying intent;
- store evidence and unknowns in the CRM;
- keep AI-created leads in `researching` until founder review.

Natasha must not send external messages, approve drafts, promise pricing/timelines/performance/SLA, or mark a lead `qualified` solely from an AI score.

Default research batch: 3 companies. The daily API cap is a safety limit, not a target.

## 6. Pipeline stages
- `new`: account discovered; source URL captured.
- `researching`: public evidence being checked.
- `qualified`: founder confirms ICP fit, a specific use case, buyer path and reason to engage.
- `contacted`: actual message sent; channel/date logged.
- `replied`: human response received.
- `meeting`: discovery call booked or held; record which.
- `proposal`: written scope and commercial proposal sent.
- `pilot`: paid scope accepted in writing and kickoff conditions agreed.
- `won`: signed agreement or confirmed paid order.
- `lost`: opportunity closed; reason recorded.
- `disqualified`: clear mismatch or no credible buying path.

A message sent is not traction. A reply is not a qualified opportunity. A proposal is not revenue. A signed SOW is not cash received.

## 7. Outreach quality gate
Before drafting, answer:
1. What exact service, product or active project makes this account relevant?
2. What operational gap could Varia plausibly fill?
3. Is the gap complementary rather than a direct competitor's core offer?
4. Can the buyer be identified?
5. Is there a reason to contact now?
6. Can Varia deliver the proposed outcome today?
7. Does the message ask about a paid business problem, not free model validation?

If fewer than 5/7 are satisfied, do not draft outreach.

One company-specific observation, one proposed engineering angle, one low-friction ask. No generic product pitch. No free custom report. No "just checking in". For new contacts, at most one value-adding follow-up after a reasonable response window; then stop unless they engage. Suppress prospects with multiple prior follow-ups.

## 9. CRM minimum standard
Every active lead needs a linked company, canonical website, country/segment, source URL/date, factual fit rationale, known unknowns, founder-reviewed stage, offer route, one next action/date, and a contact record or explicit contact-research action.

Every actual sent message must create an activity and dated next action. Never create a follow-up without a real next step.

## 10. Proposal and delivery safeguards
Every proposal states the problem/current workflow, exact scope/data volume, deliverables/acceptance criteria, client dependencies/data rights, timeline/review windows, fee/currency/payment milestones, exclusions/change control/support boundaries, IP/confidentiality/retention/deletion terms.

Default posture: paid diagnostic or implementation sprint; kickoff payment before work starts; no free custom reports, unlimited revisions, or unsupported field/agronomic/carbon-credit/MRV/yield/chemical-savings claims.

Contracting, payment eligibility, sanctions/export controls, taxes and cross-border data transfer must be checked for the actual legal parties before signing or transferring customer data.

## 11. Weekly metrics
Track accounts researched and founder-reviewed, disqualified/duplicate accounts, verified buyer contacts, first touches, positive replies, meetings booked/held, scoped opportunities, proposals, signed contracts, cash received, delivery hours, gross margin and loss reasons.

Primary KPI: qualified paid opportunity and cash collected — not research volume or email count.

## 12. First-customer milestone
A first customer is a repeatable proof point: paid scope agreed; payment received; work delivered against acceptance criteria; client confirms the result; permission obtained for a reference/case study; delivery effort and margin recorded. Only after comparable paid engagements should Varia productize a service or commit to subscriptions.
